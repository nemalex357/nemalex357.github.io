StitchChat Support

StitchChat stitches screenshots of a chat into one tall picture, without repeats and without the messenger's bars. Turn on "Hide personal details" and it covers names, avatars, phone numbers, emails, @usernames, links and faces with opaque patches. Everything runs on your phone; nothing is uploaded anywhere.

Frequently asked questions

A seam is marked "check" — why?

The app joins two screenshots where it finds the same messages in both. When it is not sure, it shows an amber banner instead of guessing silently. The usual reasons: the two screenshots share too little (you scrolled more than one screen between them), the overlapping part is a list of identical messages (a broadcast or a sticker chain), or one screenshot is not from the same conversation. Tap the banner to see the seam up close and fix it by hand, or send the picture as is. Next time, scroll about a third of a screen less, so neighbouring screenshots share a good chunk.

Why are the patches pink?

Pink is not decoration — it is arithmetic. A patch has to stay visible on any chat background: on blue and green bubbles, in light and dark themes, and for people with impaired colour vision. A blue or purple patch dissolves into the bubbles, red argues with system warnings, grey looks like a glitch. We tested fifteen messenger backgrounds and the two most common forms of colour blindness; fuchsia was the only colour that stayed visible everywhere. Patches are always opaque: blur and pixelation can be reversed, paint cannot.

Some names in a group chat were not hidden

In group chats, sender names appear in many places — above bubbles, inside quoted replies, in "X added Y" lines, in the text itself — and the app looks for all of them. It is still the hardest case, and a short or unusual name can slip through. Always look through the picture before sending it. If a name stays open, write to us with the messenger and the theme (light or dark) — without the screenshot itself unless you are comfortable sharing it. That is how we improve the detection.

How do I send the picture without compression?

Messengers shrink tall pictures: WhatsApp and Telegram reduce the long side to roughly 1600 pixels, and the text becomes blurry. Tap Share and choose "As an uncompressed file": the app sends a one-page PDF with the full-resolution picture, and the messenger passes it on as a file, untouched. "As one picture" (PNG) is for the chat feed, where a thumbnail is enough. The app remembers your last choice.

Where did my screenshots go?

Nowhere. The originals stay in Photos exactly as they were — the app can only add new pictures there, it cannot see or change your library. While you work, copies live in the app's temporary folder; they are deleted when you close the result or start a new one. The finished picture appears in Photos only when you save it yourself.

How do I turn off notifications?

The app sends only one kind of notification: "Job saved — tap to open StitchChat", after the share extension has handed a job to the app. To turn it off, open iOS Settings → Notifications → StitchChat and switch off Allow Notifications. Nothing else changes.

The background looks different above and below a seam (Telegram)

Telegram's animated and gradient wallpapers shift between screenshots, so the wallpaper on one screenshot never matches the next one exactly. The messages are joined correctly; only the backdrop changes at the seam. If it bothers you, set a plain wallpaper in Telegram before taking the screenshots.

A single large picture from the share sheet says "Open StitchChat"

The share extension has very little memory. A very tall picture (for example, a result you already stitched) does not fit, so the extension asks you to pick it in the app instead. The app handles it without trouble.
